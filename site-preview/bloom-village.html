Our Heart

Bloom Village was created from love. It was inspired by the compassion of a mother who believed in serving others and by the dream of a husband who envisioned a place where adults with intellectual and developmental disabilities could continue to grow, build friendships and live with dignity.

Every person who walks through our doors becomes part of that story. We are not simply operating a program. We are building a community.

🌱 Our Vision

A welcoming community where adults with intellectual and developmental disabilities can continue learning, connecting, creating and growing throughout adulthood.

🎯 Our Mission

Bloom Village provides a safe, welcoming adult enrichment center where adults with intellectual and developmental disabilities can build friendships, learn life skills, enjoy meaningful activities and continue growing throughout adulthood, while giving families peace of mind.

Who We Serve

Bloom Village is planned for adults age 18 and older with intellectual and/or developmental disabilities who are interested in meaningful activities and social connection. Admission depends on each adult's needs and whether we can support them safely. A diagnosis alone never decides admission.

A good fit

  • Adults 18+ with I/DD who enjoy activities and social connection
  • Able to take part in a small group with the support we can safely provide
  • Eats and uses the restroom independently, or with help our staff are trained and available to give
  • Communication, health, sensory, movement and behavior support needs our team can meet

What we are, and are not

  • Adult enrichment, individualized skills and socialization
  • Not skilled nursing, clinical treatment or continuous medical care
  • Staff do not give, store, hand out or remind members to take medicine at launch
  • On-site activities at opening; no outings planned at launch

When we may not be able to serve

  • Needs skilled nursing, continuous medical monitoring or support outside our scope
  • Eating/swallowing, toileting, mobility, communication, behavior, seizure or other safety needs our staff and procedures cannot meet
  • Needs continuous one-to-one support we cannot provide at that time
  • A risk that cannot be managed safely with reasonable supports and changes

We never exclude someone only because of a diagnosis. We review each person's needs, discuss reasonable supports, record the decision and explain the next step respectfully.

Small and careful

Our three-month pilot will serve up to 8 adults, 2–3 days per week, only within the HHSC-licensed capacity, building occupancy and safe staffing. We discuss reasonable supports and explain every decision respectfully.

Our Space

One calm, accessible 23 ft × 23 ft activity room, planned around adults' interests. The room plan is a concept and will be finalized with City approvals.

🎨 Arts, crafts & music wall

Art display rail, instruments and closed storage.

🧩 Sensory wall & mini library

Safe sensory panels, low shelves and two comfortable reading seats.

🍽️ Life-skills counter

A counter and sink for simple, supervised everyday tasks. No hot appliances or knives without a documented safety review.

🧸 Quiet area

Three recliners, teddy bears and headphones for calm breaks.

🪑 Flexible tables

Two semi-circle tables seating 5–6 each, on locking wheels so they can be joined.

📺 Visual supports

A picture notice board (schedule, "How I feel", "I need", choices, weather/date) and a 65-inch TV for schedules, music and movement.

How Admission Works: 7 Steps

  1. Initial inquiry and a brief conversation about fit and program scope.
  2. Facility tour and a communication and accessibility check.
  3. Enrollment and confidential health, allergy and support forms.
  4. Welcome conversation with the adult participant and legal representative, if applicable.
  5. Introductory visit, only after the right permissions are in place and the HHSC license is issued.
  6. Administrator review of support needs, staffing, risks and required documents.
  7. Written admission decision and an individual enrichment and activity plan.

A legal representative is someone legally allowed to make certain decisions for an adult, such as a court-appointed guardian. Being a parent or relative alone does not automatically give that authority, and we involve each adult in their own decisions as much as possible.

The Bloom Journey

Four pillars keep activities meaningful, respectful for adults, and matched to each person's strengths and goals.

🌿 GROW

Everyday independence and confidence. Life skills, simple cooking tasks, gardening, organizing, safe movement and exercise, handwashing routines.

🤝 CONNECT

Friendship, communication and belonging. Morning circle, cooperative games, conversation prompts, music sharing, turn-taking, group celebrations.

🎨 CREATE

Expression, imagination and fine-motor practice. Arts and crafts, painting, collage, LEGO/building wall, seasonal projects, digital art.

🌸 THRIVE

Choice, wellness and meaningful participation. Music, sensory exploration, games, reading, personal choices, community participation, quiet breaks.

Every day we use a picture-based notice board and a visual schedule, and we respect speech, pictures, gestures, communication devices and every preferred way of communicating.

Licensing Status

Licensing status: Bloom Village has applied to the Texas Health and Human Services Commission (HHSC) for a Day Activity and Health Services license, Individualized Skills & Socialization Only (application APP-0062670597). The license is pending. Bloom Village services will begin only after HHSC issues the license and lists Bloom Village in its directory. A pending application is not permission to operate. Until then we are taking inquiries and scheduling tours, and we will contact families as soon as an opening date is confirmed.

Separately from HHSC, the City of Carrollton must approve the space (permits, inspections and Certificate of Occupancy). We will post the issued license and HHSC complaint information on site once licensed.

Rights, Privacy & Concerns

  • Each adult and legal representative receives a written rights list, explained in a way the person understands.
  • Members can make choices, ask for help or a break, and decline an activity.
  • Members and families have an easy way to raise a concern or complaint, without retaliation. Call (469) 270-8156.
  • Health and enrollment records are kept confidential and shared only as authorized or required by law.
  • Photo and video permission is separate and optional; saying no never affects enrollment. Photos on this page are illustrations, not current members.
  • Staff report suspected abuse, neglect or exploitation to HHSC right away, as Texas rules require.

Volunteer at Bloom Village

Kind, patient adults who enjoy arts, music, games, gardening or simply good conversation are welcome. Volunteers support, but never replace, our trained staff.

Direct-support volunteers must be 18+, have a high school diploma/equivalent or pass a written proficiency evaluation with three non-relative references, complete required background/registry screening and training (CPR, first aid, infection control, abuse reporting), and always work under a designated supervisor.

FAQ

When will Bloom Village open?

After HHSC issues our license. The application is pending, so we have no opening date yet. Send an inquiry and we'll contact you.

Who can attend?

Adults 18 and older with intellectual and/or developmental disabilities, when we can safely meet their support needs.

What does it cost?

$30 per program day plus a one-time $100 non-refundable enrollment fee. Need-based scholarships of 25%, 50%, 75% or up to 100% are available as funds allow.

Do you give medication or nursing care?

No. Bloom Village is adult enrichment, not skilled nursing or clinical care. At launch, staff do not give, store, hand out or remind members to take medicine.

Are there outings or community trips?

Not at launch. Bloom Village activities are on-site only at opening; no outings or community trips are planned.
